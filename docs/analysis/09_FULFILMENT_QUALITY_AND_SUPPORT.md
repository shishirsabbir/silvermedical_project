# Fulfilment quality and support

Baseline: 7 November 2026. This is a proposed operating design. Exact controls depend on product instructions, regulatory role and contracted service levels.

## Inventory ownership and promises

Publish separate availability for own warehouse stock, supplier-direct stock, backorders and quotation products. Available-to-promise must deduct allocated, quarantined, damaged, expired and otherwise restricted stock. A positive supplier feed is not guaranteed order acceptance.

Define postcode/service coverage, order cutoff, handling days, dispatch date and delivery estimate separately. Treat weekends, bank holidays, remote postcodes, bulky freight, hazardous goods, installation and split shipments explicitly. A next-day claim needs a product/postcode-specific basis and a stock reservation at checkout.

## Receiving and storage

Check receipt against PO, exact SKU/variant, count, lot/serial, expiry, packaging and transport condition. Quarantine discrepancies until resolved. Record manufacturer storage limits by product and use monitoring, mapping and excursion handling appropriate to those limits. Do not invent one universal storage-temperature range for all medical goods.

Use first-expiry-first-out where appropriate, with customer minimum-shelf-life promises built into allocation. Separate sterile integrity, chemical hazards, cold chain and returned stock. Keep lot-level stock locations and an audit trail for manual adjustments.

For medicines, align the operation with the applicable GDP/licence requirements, approved premises and responsible personnel rather than assuming an ordinary ecommerce 3PL is sufficient. [MHRA medicines licensing](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-manufacturer-or-wholesaler-of-medicines-licences).

For chemicals, lithium batteries, aerosols, gases or other hazardous shipments, determine the actual classification and permitted road/air/courier route, including return transport. Carrier acceptance is an operational gate. [Dangerous goods overview](https://www.gov.uk/moving-dangerous-goods).

## Traceability and recalls

Proposed minimum trace chain:

    Supplier / manufacturer → purchase order → goods receipt → lot or serial → warehouse position → sales order line → shipment → customer organisation/address

Keep returns, replacements and service exchanges connected to the original lot/serial. A supplier-direct agreement must return the same trace data. Record the legal basis and retention period by product/role; do not assume one retention period applies to devices, medicines, tax and marketing data alike.

Maintain complaint intake, safety triage, escalation, stock blocking, affected-order queries, customer notification preparation, retrieval, reconciliation and effectiveness checks. Run a mock recall before sales in relevant categories. Manufacturers have specific PMS duties; the reseller workflow needs to cooperate with them and any authority obligations that apply to its own role. [MHRA PMS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-post-market-surveillance-requirements/introduction-and-scope).

## Returns and service

Separate consumer cancellations, commercial returns, damaged deliveries, faults, warranty cases and safety complaints. Returned clinical products must be assessed before restocking; a visually intact box alone may not establish saleable integrity. Do not accept contaminated used items into an ordinary returns lane.

For equipment, price delivery access checks, installation, commissioning, user training, calibration, planned maintenance, spare parts, loan units, manufacturer-authorised repair and end-of-life support. Obtain model-specific terms before advertising a warranty. Outsourced service still needs a named case owner and escalation route.

## Customer support design

Use one order history and named case ownership across email, phone and chat. Distinguish product selection information from clinical advice. Train support to escalate suitability, medicine, adverse-event and patient-safety questions to the right professional/operator.

Trigger proactive communication for missed dispatch, supplier rejection, split orders, short dates and delayed refunds. Show a credible revised date and customer options, rather than repeating generic explanations. Contact details and company identity should be consistent across the store, invoices and third-party profiles.

## Measures and proposed acceptance standards

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Line fill rate | Eligible ordered units supplied from the promised source without backorder |
| On-time in-full | Orders delivered complete within the agreed customer promise |
| Pick accuracy | Correct product, variant, quantity and lot/date requirements |
| Promise accuracy | Actual dispatch/delivery against the dates shown at purchase |
| Stock accuracy | Counted usable stock versus system usable stock |
| Shelf-life compliance | Delivered lots meeting the customer's promised remaining life |
| Service resolution | Time from case opening to effective resolution, not first reply |
| Refund completion | Time to completed payment-provider refund, with bank timing distinguished |
| Recall readiness | Ability to identify and reconcile affected stock/orders in a timed drill |

Targets must be agreed with warehouse/carrier capabilities and measured in the pilot. No service benchmark is asserted as achieved.
