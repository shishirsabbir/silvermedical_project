# European expansion

Baseline: 7 November 2026. Europe is the confirmed longer-term ambition. No first expansion country, EU entity, warehouse or importer route has been selected.

## Treat markets separately

Europe is not a single regulatory/customs territory. Separate EU Member States, EEA countries outside the EU, Switzerland and other destinations. NI is also a distinct UK device-market case. Choose countries using validated buyer demand, supplier rights, language/service capability, landed contribution, payment behaviour and fulfilment complexity.

Do not call a UK-authorised product automatically EU-authorised. Review CE/conformity evidence, transition eligibility, labels/IFU language and manufacturer/representative/importer responsibilities for the destination and actual transaction.

## Device-market roles

EU MDR/IVDR distinguish manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers, distributors and pack producers. Allocate those roles explicitly. Selling directly from GB does not eliminate the need for an applicable EU economic-operator arrangement. A customs importer and a medical-device importer are related questions but must not be assumed identical in every commercial structure. [European Commission economic operators](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medical-devices-topics-interest/economic-operators_en).

Review Articles 13/14 and repackaging/translation Article 16 activities with the actual manufacturer and compliance adviser. EUDAMED obligations depend on role, device and transition dates; not every distributor needs the same registration. Use current Commission guidance and the NI/EU registration rules rather than a blanket website requirement. [Commission guidance index](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medical-devices-sector/new-regulations/guidance-mdcg-endorsed-documents-and-other-guidance_en).

Medicines, biocides and veterinary products need separate country authorisation and professional supply analysis. Device CE status cannot authorise those categories. [EU biocides overview](https://health.ec.europa.eu/biocides/biocidal-products_en).

## Compare three fulfilment models

| Model | Potential advantage | Main questions |
|---|---|---|
| GB stock → EU buyer | One initial inventory pool | Border cost, importer, duty, VAT, delivery friction and expensive returns |
| EU supplier → EU buyer | Avoids routing all stock through GB | Seller/importer roles, supplier reliability, local tax and traceability |
| EU warehouse → EU buyer | Domestic/regional service and returns | Local registrations, inventory VAT, operating cost and stock duplication |

Cost each country/category lane rather than assuming the GB model scales unchanged. Currency conversion and translation alone do not make a store ready for a new market.

## VAT and customs

Separate B2B from B2C, goods held in EU stock from goods imported per parcel, and Union OSS from IOSS. IOSS is a conditional mechanism for eligible non-excise imported consumer consignments not exceeding EUR150; it is not a general B2B scheme or a replacement for every local VAT obligation. EU inventory may create local registration/reporting duties. [Commission IOSS conditions](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-directive/vat-exemptions/other-exemptions_en).

Do not use the old assumption that all EU imports under EUR150 are duty-free. Commission guidance describes a temporary EUR3 customs duty from 1 July 2026 for affected low-value distance-sale imports, with calculation, declaration and preferential-origin conditions. Obtain current tariff/broker confirmation for each route and clarify what constitutes an item in the customs calculation. Do not multiply EUR3 mechanically by physical units or confuse this duty with a proposed handling fee. [Commission June 2026 guidance](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/guidance-and-legal-text-temporary-flat-fee-low-value-imports-which-will-apply-until-1-july-2028-2026-06-08_en).

UK/EU origin preference requires proof and qualifying origin; buying from an EU supplier is not enough. Avoid unnecessary GB re-export routing until total cost and possible reliefs have been validated. [HMRC origin guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/general-rules-to-determine-the-origin-your-products-for-trade-between-the-uk-and-eu).

## Localisation and consumer requirements

Prepare country-appropriate product instructions, safety notices, terms, tax display, invoices, delivery promises, local payments, support and return arrangements. Review general product-safety rules for ordinary goods and uncovered risks rather than treating the catalogue as entirely outside them. Category-specific legislation may govern the main medical product obligations. [Commission product safety](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/business-and-industry/product-safety_en).

EU consumer ecommerce services fall within the European Accessibility Act framework from 28 June 2025, subject to national implementation and relevant exemptions, including for qualifying microenterprises providing services. Assess actual applicability, and design accessible flows regardless. [Directive 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj).

Review packaging EPR, WEEE and battery obligations by destination and seller role. The Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation began applying in stages from 12 August 2026; NI has specific treatment. Do not assume every obligation or future target started on the same date. [DAERA PPWR explanation](https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/articles/packaging-and-packaging-waste).

## Country release gate

Use a [country dossier](../templates/COUNTRY_LAUNCH_DOSSIER.md). Required decisions: approved categories/SKUs, operator roles, tax/customs route, language/evidence, resale rights, shipping/returns, warranty service, packaging/environmental duties, privacy/marketing, accessibility and contribution/cash. Activate a country only after owners sign off the actual lane. This baseline is not a completed legal dossier for any EU country.
