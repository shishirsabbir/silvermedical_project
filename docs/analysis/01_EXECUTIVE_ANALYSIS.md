# Executive analysis

Baseline: 7 November 2026. User decisions: new brand, UK first, Europe later, full product scope and mixed UK/EU sourcing.

Update 8 November: the user is the commissioned developer for the owners, and confirms a non-dropshipping supplier. The supplier-direct options below were early recommendations, not accepted requirements; do not adopt them as the fulfilment model. Latest brief: [developer clarification](../../memory/BRIEF_2026-11-08.md). Implementation is not starting from this discussion.

The business should be designed as a professional procurement operation with an ecommerce front end. Its economics depend on repeat baskets and reliable fulfilment; its ability to sell depends on the product, intended use, customer, supply-chain role and destination. A large catalogue alone does not establish either.

Praxisdienst offers useful patterns: specialised navigation, manufacturer identifiers, saved lists, repeat ordering and professional support. Its published terms target professional buyers. Those observations support a procurement-focused design; they do not establish the new business's buyer restrictions or legal terms. [Praxisdienst](https://www.praxisdienst.com/), [FAQ](https://www.praxisdienst.com/en-en/information-pages/contact/frequently-asked-questions-faqs).

## Business model to evaluate

Use a broad catalogue with three explicitly different fulfilment promises: stock physically available at the nominated UK warehouse; supplier-direct items with confirmed supplier dispatch rules; and quotation-only or approval-only products. Suppliers in both the UK and EU remain within scope. Each route needs its own landed cost, service level and role allocation.

The proposed rollout has parallel workstreams: ordinary and document-ready products; device/PPE/chemical routes; installation and service-heavy equipment; and licensed medicines or other specialist activities. This preserves the full-range ambition while making readiness visible. No category is authorised merely because it exists in the taxonomy.

## The commercial offer

Test a promise centred on dependable replenishment: correct products and pack sizes, clear VAT-inclusive totals where consumers can buy, credible stock and dates, usable documentation, quick reordering and reachable support. A low headline price loses value when buyers receive the wrong pack, a short-dated lot, unannounced substitutions or unexpected freight charges.

Use known branded products to establish comparability. Test margin on the complete delivered basket, including bulky paper products, liquid freight, split shipments and service costs. Add private label only after its manufacturer obligations, testing, insurance and economics have been costed.

## Critical choices

| Choice | Consequence |
|---|---|
| B2B-only versus mixed B2B/B2C | Account verification, price presentation, terms and marketing |
| UK distributor versus importer per SKU | Different registration/evidence and customs workflows |
| Own stock versus supplier-direct | Cash tied up versus availability/control tradeoff |
| Resale versus private label/repacking | Potential manufacturer or pack-producer responsibilities |
| Retail medicines versus wholesale supply | Different professional, premises and licence routes |
| GB-only activation versus NI too | Destination-specific release rules |
| GB-to-EU parcels versus EU inventory | Landed margin, importer allocation, VAT and returns |

## What must be proven before scaling

1. Customers will place repeat orders for an approved assortment.
2. Suppliers provide valid evidence, genuine products, resale rights, service support and sustainable costs.
3. Each category, SKU and destination passes its release requirements.
4. The delivered order generates contribution after variable costs and a realistic acquisition allowance.
5. Stock investment, payment timing and customer credit fit the cash budget.
6. Product complaints and recalls can be traced to affected orders promptly.

The immediate research priority is supplier quotations plus a like-for-like competitor basket, supported by customer interviews. UK-wide market revenue, margins and conversion rates have not been established and must not be inferred from competitor catalogue size.
