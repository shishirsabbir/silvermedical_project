Business and scopePDF page 3

The business we are building

Build a substantial UK medical supplies retailer with its own stock, under a new brand. The website, admin and API support a real procurement and fulfilment operation.

The owners selected a single-retailer model: the business purchases goods, receives and holds stock, sells directly and remains responsible for the customer order. Mixed UK/EU sourcing may include Praxisdienst and original manufacturers. No supplier or content agreement is established by this plan.

AreaProject direction
CustomersConsumers and verified professional/business buyers; product entitlement is checked separately.
RangeFull-range ambition across supplies, equipment, diagnostics, PPE, infection control and relevant medicine/veterinary routes. Actual SKUs are unknown.
MarketUK first. Great Britain and Northern Ireland need separate eligibility decisions; Europe follows country by country.
BenchmarkPraxisdienst is the operating/customer-experience benchmark. Its catalogue is not a UK permission or a content licence.
Operating identityNew brand and legal operator still to be confirmed. The workspace name does not establish either.

How an ordinary sale should work

Qualify a supplier, approve the exact product and destination, receive usable stock, publish accurate information, accept an eligible order, reserve stock, confirm payment, dispatch and support the customer. Refunds, returns and recalls remain part of the same record.

Definition of success

A buyer can find the correct item and pack, see the complete price, order with an approved method and receive the promised goods. Staff can explain every hold, stock movement and refund.

Deliberate initial simplifications

Manual carrier booking and tracking entry, accounting exports and human professional verification are acceptable if documented, staffed and tested. They must be described honestly and must not bypass product, tax or payment controls.

Complete means usable end to end

An attractive storefront with unfinished refunds, stock or dispatch handling does not meet the initial selling requirement. The acceptance scenarios later in this document define completion.

UK selling requirementsPDF page 6

Medical product rules in the UK

There is no single universal medical-supplies licence. Determine the product's intended purpose, claims, buyer, destination and the retailer's actual supply-chain role.

RouteReview required before activation
Devices and IVDsGB: applicable UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 conformity/registration route. Retain relevant declarations, labels, instructions and required certificates/operator evidence. [R01-R02]
Northern IrelandReview EU MDR/IVDR device arrangements, applicable marking and role-specific registration separately. A GB decision is not an NI decision. [R03]
PPEAssess wearer-protection purpose, conformity and operator duties. Dual-purpose items can need both PPE and device routes. [R04]
BiocidesEstablish product boundary, permitted claims and applicable authorisation/transitional route. GB BPR and NI EU BPR differ. [R05]
Human medicinesSeparate GSL, pharmacy/POM, patient supply, wholesale and import activities; see the dedicated decision page. [R06-R08]
Veterinary medicinesVMD distribution category, authorised product, professional/premises and internet-sale requirements; NI qualifications matter. [R09]
Other/specialist goodsAssess ordinary product safety, electrical/chemical/transport rules, sharps, gases, radiation, servicing and environmental duties as applicable.

Current GB CE recognition has conditional transitions: eligible legacy MDD/AIMDD products to certificate expiry or 30 June 2028, eligible IVDD products to certificate expiry or 30 June 2030, and MDR/IVDR products to 30 June 2030. Exceptions and transition conditions apply. Proposed indefinite recognition is not enacted permission. [R01]

What the software should enforce

Store classification, evidence revision, seller role, buyer and destination permissions, review dates and stop-sale status. Registration is not MHRA endorsement; the platform must not display it as approval. [R02]

UK selling requirementsPDF page 7

Medicines and restricted purchasing

Prescription-only medicines remain an open business decision. A prescription upload field or a professional account does not establish an authorised supply operation.

ActivityDecision and evidence needed
Patient supplyAppropriate pharmacy/professional model, premises where required, lawful prescribing/dispensing and accountable oversight. Establish GB/NI scope.
Supply to clinicsWholesale activity assessment, relevant WDA(H) or applicable exception, premises/personnel, good distribution practice and customer entitlement.
Importing medicinesOrigin/activity-specific authorisations and responsibilities. An EU supplier invoice does not settle the medicine import route.
Controlled drugs/clinical serviceSeparate specialist assessment of additional permissions and professional/healthcare-service responsibilities.

The legal and operational assessment must cover the exact activities rather than treating every sale to a medical customer alike. Relevant exceptions require confirmation; a licensed partner's permissions do not automatically cover this retailer. [R06-R07]

Buyer verification and order release

Separate business verification, a person's purchasing authority, profession-specific entitlement and any medicine supply review. A professional-use device is not automatically a prescription medicine. Store the reason, reviewer, evidence and expiry of each approval; recheck the effective rules at checkout and dispatch where necessary.

If a future prescription workflow is accepted, use private documents, named authorised reviewers, access logs and a retention policy. Do not send clinical documents through public R2 asset URLs or routine marketing exports.

Publication and advertising gate

Recommended: keep prescription-medicine activation disabled until the owners substantiate the operating route. Public POM advertising is prohibited; professional promotion has separate audience/content requirements. [R08]

UK selling requirementsPDF page 8

Consumer rights, pricing and VAT

Consumer and professional purchasing share infrastructure, but contract status, product entitlement and tax treatment must be assessed independently.

What customers must understand

Show the actual seller, contact/address details, accurate product information, complete prices and payment/delivery terms. Explain cancellation and returns before purchase, allow error correction and provide a durable confirmation. Consumer prices must account for VAT and unavoidable charges; handle variable delivery charges transparently. [C01,C03]

For goods within ordinary distance-cancellation rules, consumers generally have 14 days after receipt to notify cancellation and another 14 days to return. Apply refund timing and standard-delivery rules. A qualifying sealed hygiene exception applies after unsealing; it is not a blanket medical-products exclusion. Faulty-goods rights remain separate. [C02]

How tax is implemented

The UK standard VAT rate is 20%. The ordinary UK-established registration tests include taxable turnover above £90,000 over the previous 12 months or expected above £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. Non-established operators can face different rules. [C04-C05]

Use product/transaction tax categories. Disability relief and charity-funded equipment relief have separate qualifying-goods, recipient/use/funding and evidence conditions. An NHS address, professional account or declaration does not make every item zero-rated. Obtain accountant approval for the actual rules, mixed baskets, delivery and imports. [C06-C07]

Policies to prepare

Consumer terms, commercial terms, delivery, cancellation/returns, warranty/service, complaints, privacy/tracking and restricted-product policies. Review against CCR 2013, CRA 2015, Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002 and current DMCC 2024 requirements; preserve version/acceptance records.

The admin is the operating workspace for the retailer. Its first priority is helping staff act correctly and see why an item or order is blocked.

WorkspaceCapabilities
Work queuesProducts awaiting evidence, verification applications, incoming receipts, quarantined lots, held orders, failed jobs and reconciliation issues.
CatalogueDraft imports with previews/errors, variants and pack units, document revisions, permissions, price/tax codes and upload optimisation.

React implementation

Use regular React with a proposed Vite/TypeScript setup. The browser calls the API and never connects directly to PostgreSQL. Shared API contracts, server-side pagination/filtering, indexed lookups and background imports keep large screens responsive.

Inventory rules

Separate available, reserved and quarantined stock. Reserve and release through transactions; avoid a single freely edited stock number. Returns are inspected before becoming saleable. Add richer purchase-order approvals, multi-location allocation, cycle counts, automated supplier feeds and deeper warehouse systems after the core stock/order record is reliable.

Product and customer experiencePDF page 12

Orders, fulfilment and after-sales

An order must remain understandable from basket through payment, dispatch, returns and reconciliation.

StepControl and responsible action
CheckoutAPI recalculates price/tax/delivery, validates product/buyer/destination and reserves the appropriate stock. Snapshot the accepted terms and totals.
PaymentCreate a pending payment/order record. Verify provider events and update state idempotently; a browser success redirect is not sufficient proof.
ReleaseResolve professional/supply holds and confirm stock/payment. Recheck affected orders if eligibility or stock status changes.
DispatchPick the correct pack/lot, record shipment/tracking and communicate the actual delivery promise. Define partial dispatch and restricted mixed-basket handling.
Return/refundRecord reason, statutory/commercial route, inspection/quarantine, refund/credit note and payment reconciliation.
Safety/serviceEscalate complaints and adverse-event concerns; trace affected orders. Equipment installation, repair and calibration need a named provider/owner.

Fulfilment arrangements to establish

Owners must provide premises or an appropriate stock operation, trained staff, storage/temperature requirements, suitable carriers, packaging and returns handling. Bulky, hazardous or temperature-sensitive goods need their actual routes and pricing rather than a universal parcel promise.

Customer support

Link email/web/phone cases to orders, assign ownership and set staffed response hours. Distinguish factual product information from clinical advice and route safety or suitability questions to the appropriate professional/operator.

Acceptance example

Staff can explain a held order, locate its stock, resolve the hold, dispatch it, issue a partial refund and reconcile the final amount without editing database records manually.

Payments and growthPDF page 14

Google Merchant Center and campaigns

Lawful website supply, Merchant Center listings and Google Ads are separate eligibility decisions. Build controlled channel rules into the catalogue.

UK prescription/behind-the-counter drug listings are excluded from Merchant Center. Its UK OTC route requires a GPhC-registered online pharmacy certified by Google. Other healthcare goods need individual review, and Google can assess the website/accounts as well as submitted products. [M01]

Google Ads has separate UK online-pharmacy conditions and certification; prescription drugs cannot be promoted in the relevant UK pharmacy ads/landing pages. Apply UK medicines advertising law as well. [M02,R08]

Onboarding sequence

  • Obtain the former store's actual notices, affected products/entities and appeal status. The reported ban/closure causes are unverified; a new name does not resolve underlying enforcement.
  • Use truthful operator/domain/contact information and clear delivery, payment and returns terms. Review the whole launch site before submitting a feed.
  • Verify/claim the domain, configure required business/shipping/returns settings, and submit only reviewed, available, purchasable items with genuine identifiers and consistent prices/stock.
  • Resolve diagnostic causes, follow the applicable review process and monitor product/policy changes. Approval cannot be guaranteed. [M03-M04]

Campaign approach

Start with original category/product SEO and tightly scoped Search/Shopping for eligible stocked goods with known contribution. Review claims, search terms, landing pages, exclusions and spend. Evaluate Performance Max after feed controls work, including URL expansion and automated assets.

Measurement and targeting

Reconcile conversions to paid orders/refunds and contribution after advertising. Do not build audiences from prescriptions or inferred health conditions; review sensitive-interest targeting rules before activating remarketing/customer lists. [M05]

Privacy, security and trust

Medical ecommerce should minimise sensitive information and maintain clear access, retention and processing responsibilities.

Data and tracking

Document UK GDPR/DPA 2018 purposes, lawful bases, retention, processor contracts and international transfer arrangements. Identifiable health information requires both an Article 6 basis and an Article 9 condition, with associated UK conditions where required. Do not collect prescriptions or condition details for ordinary supply orders by default. [P03]

Block advertising tracking until valid consent. Review any applicable statistical-purpose exception against current ICO conditions; permission to receive marketing is not blanket permission for tracking pixels or advertising audiences. [P04]

ControlProposed implementation
Staff accessMFA, least-privilege roles, secure sessions, access reviews and rapid offboarding. Separate review and operational responsibilities where needed.
DocumentsPrivate storage and short-lived authorised access for sensitive records; audit access and exports. No public image URLs for clinical evidence.
InfrastructurePrivate PostgreSQL, restricted network access, secret management, patching, validated uploads and rate/payload limits.
ApplicationAPI validation/authorisation, CSRF/session protection as appropriate, server totals, idempotency, safe logs and dependency review.

Architecture and hosting design

Host the application services on Hostinger KVM and use Cloudflare for the domain and R2 objects. This supersedes the earlier Cloudflare frontend plan.

LayerTechnology and responsibility
Public storefrontNext.js on Hostinger; product discovery, rendering, basket and account UI.
AdminRegular React; Vite/TypeScript recommended, not selected by the user. Browser calls the API.
APINestJS with Fastify recommended; plain Fastify is an alternative. One authoritative business service, initially a modular monolith.
DataPostgreSQL, transactions/constraints and migrations. Prisma is a proposed access layer; ORM choice remains open.
WorkerDurable imports, image optimisation, notifications, exports and feed jobs with limits/retries. Choose a queue after workload review.
CloudflareRegistrar/DNS and R2 prepared media. Keep private records separate from public assets.
IntegrationsApproved payment providers, managed email and later accounting/carrier systems; verified events and reconciliation.

Deployment topology

Internet -> reverse proxy -> Next.js / static React admin / API. The API and worker use private PostgreSQL and permitted R2/provider connections. Use distinct service users, secrets and environments. Keep builds and resource-heavy jobs from starving the production database.

Nest supports a Fastify adapter; integration packages must suit the adapter. Next.js self-hosting guidance covers a reverse proxy, cache persistence and multi-instance coordination. Framework choice alone does not establish throughput. [T09-T11]

KVM 4 capacity is a hypothesis

The published starting plan has 4 vCPU, 16GB RAM, 200GB NVMe and 16TB bandwidth. Measure combined rendering/API/database/worker peaks. One VPS remains a shared outage point even with isolated services. [T01]

Use clear domain modules and shared API contracts so UI changes do not bypass stock, approval, pricing or payment rules.

Build a representative vertical slice first

Demonstrate one reviewed product through admin upload, stock receipt, storefront, eligible checkout, provider sandbox event, dispatch and refund. Record actual effort and failures before extrapolating to the whole backlog. This tests architecture and integration decisions together.

Where implementation effort should go

Reuse established authentication/payment/UI components where appropriate. Keep medical approval, owned-stock traceability and effective buyer/destination rules explicit in our domain. Background jobs must be bounded, observable and safe to retry; material supplier/evidence changes should trigger review.

AI-assisted development

AI tools can draft modules, migrations, interfaces and tests and help review changes. Human review, integration, accountable product decisions and operational checks remain required. Multiple agents do not establish a 25-day guarantee.

How to control spending

Track landed cost and contribution after fulfilment, payment, returns and advertising. Use planned cash-flow scenarios and a reserve before buying stock or extending customer credit. An illustrative £85 technology envelope is not the monthly cost of operating the retailer.

Budget decision required

Owners must approve the actual developer quote, stock/working-capital limit, operating budget and marketing test budget. No final all-in business total can be established from hosting prices alone.
